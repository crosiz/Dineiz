Choosing the right Point of Sale (POS) system is one of the most important decisions you will make for your restaurant. A good POS is the central nervous system of your business—it handles everything from punching orders and printing KOTs to managing inventory and reporting taxes.

However, the Pakistani market is flooded with options, ranging from 15-year-old desktop software to modern cloud solutions. Here is exactly what you need to look for when choosing a POS system for your restaurant in Pakistan in 2025.

## 1. True Offline Capability

This is the number one requirement for any POS in Pakistan.

Internet infrastructure is improving, but outages still happen. If your POS relies 100% on a cloud connection to punch an order, a 10-minute PTCL outage on a Saturday night will completely paralyze your kitchen.

**What to look for:**
Ensure the system has a "hybrid" or "true offline" mode. It should allow your staff to continue taking orders, printing receipts, and sending tickets to the kitchen without the internet. Once the connection is restored, it should sync the data automatically in the background.

*At Dineiz, offline mode is built into the core architecture. Your operations never stop, even if the Wi-Fi drops.*

## 2. FBR and Provincial Tax Compliance

Tax compliance in Pakistan is complex. You are not just dealing with the FBR; you are dealing with SRB in Sindh, PRA in Punjab, and KPRA in KPK.

**What to look for:**
Do not buy a POS that requires third-party plugins or manual CSV uploads to integrate with tax authorities. You need a system that is pre-integrated and certified. More importantly, it must handle dynamic tax rates (e.g., automatically applying a 5% GST for card payments and 15% for cash).

## 3. Pakistani Payment Integrations

Your customers are paying with more than just cash and Visa cards. JazzCash, EasyPaisa, SadaPay, and Nayapay are becoming the norm.

**What to look for:**
A POS that forces your cashier to manually enter JazzCash payments as "Custom Payment Method 1" will destroy your reconciliation at the end of the day. Look for a system that natively supports QR code generation on the receipt and integrates with local payment gateways for seamless reconciliation.

## 4. Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)

Many restaurant owners get trapped by "free" or incredibly cheap software, only to realize the hidden costs later.

When calculating the TCO, ask these questions:
*   **Hardware Lock-in:** Do I have to buy their expensive proprietary tablets, or can I run it on any standard Android/iPad?
*   **Setup Fees:** Is there a massive upfront installation fee?
*   **Support Fees:** Do they charge per call when something breaks on a weekend?
*   **Update Fees:** Are you forced to pay for annual "version upgrades"?

Modern cloud systems operate on transparent SaaS pricing. You pay a predictable monthly fee, and all updates, support, and backups are included.

## 5. Mobile Management (The Owner's App)

You cannot be at the restaurant 24/7. When you are sitting at home at 10 PM, you should be able to see exactly how many tables are occupied, what the total sales are, and if you are running low on a critical inventory item.

**What to look for:**
A dedicated mobile dashboard for owners that provides real-time analytics. It shouldn't just be a mobile-responsive website; it should be a fast, native-feeling app.

## Red Flags to Watch Out For

*   **"We install it on a server in your back room."** If the server crashes, your data is gone. Cloud is the standard.
*   **"Support is available Monday to Friday, 9 AM to 5 PM."** Restaurants operate on nights and weekends. Your support should too.
*   **"FBR integration costs extra."** This is a basic legal requirement; it should be included in the core product.
